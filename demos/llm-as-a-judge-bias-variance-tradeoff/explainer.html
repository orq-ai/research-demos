Bias and variance in LLM-as-a-judge, in one diagram

You have an LLM scoring outputs. Should you call it once, ten times, or call ten different models? The classical bias–variance tradeoff gives a clean answer — and the recent literature on LLM judges formalizes it. Move the sliders below and watch which error component shrinks.

The decomposition

Zhu et al. (2026) model each judge score as a sum of four random pieces: scenario difficulty (α), generation noise (β), judge bias (γ), and residual noise (ε). The benchmark mean's variance splits cleanly across these four terms.

Here's the catch: averaging more samples from one judge only shrinks α, β, ε. The γ term — the judge's systematic skew — is unchanged by repetition. You can only shrink γ by adding distinct judges.

When a single judge is fine anyway

Panels aren't always worth it. Zheng et al. (2023) showed GPT-4 alone hits >80% agreement with human raters on MT-Bench when prompts swap positions and inject reference answers. In a clinical safety eval (psychosis support), a single judge actually beat a jury against clinician consensus — the rubric was tight, the bias surface was small, and panel disagreement added more noise than it removed.

Rule of thumb: panels matter when the task is subjective, stylistic bias dominates, or cross-provider fairness is at stake. Single judges work for tight rubrics, structured outputs, and relative-delta dev iteration.

Where you'll meet it

Anywhere you're building an LLM eval pipeline that compares models. Leaderboards (MT-Bench, Chatbot Arena, MindEval) are the canonical case. Internally, the same logic applies to product eval suites, red-teaming benchmarks, and customer-facing comparison reports. The moment you publish a number across providers, γ becomes your dominant error term — and only a panel can fix it.

Glossary
Scenario (α)
The prompt or item being evaluated. Different scenarios have different inherent difficulty.
Generation (β)
Stochastic noise from the model under evaluation rolling different completions.
Judge bias (γ)
Systematic skew of one judge — position, verbosity, self-preference. Does not shrink with more samples from the same judge.
Residual (ε)
Unexplained noise per scoring call. Shrinks with everything.
PoLL
Panel of LLM Evaluators. A jury of smaller models from disjoint families, majority vote.
CyclicJudge
Round-robin assignment: each judge used equally often, never twice on the same cell.
BT-σ
Bradley-Terry with a learned per-judge reliability parameter. Handles judges of mixed quality.
