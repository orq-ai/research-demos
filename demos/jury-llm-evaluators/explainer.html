What you're feeling

1
Three judges, three providers. Different families dilute systematic bias. Same prompt to each, in parallel — not sequential.
2
TIE is a real label. When judges split, the answer is "panel ambiguous, escalate to human." Hiding it as null loses signal.
3
Disagreement = data. Unanimous on easy items, split on hard ones. The split rate is your diagnostic for prompt clarity.
4
Position swap for pairwise. Re-run hard A vs B as B vs A. Position bias is a pairwise problem — symmetrising the prompt is the cheap fix.
5
Human override is the ground truth. Your pick vs panel verdict produces the alignment signal you care about long-term.

Open questions

  • How many disagreements before re-aligning a judge? 5%? 10%? Threshold depends on stakes.
  • Weight judges by their historic agreement with humans? Or treat all votes equal (1-judge-1-vote)?
  • When does a 4th judge help? Krippendorff plateaus at 3–5. Adding more raises cost without gain.
  • Self-judge exclusion enforced where? Config layer or runtime guard?
